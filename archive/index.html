BULLISH NEPI Rockcastle (NRP) Review #1
18 Aug 2026
Reviewed Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements for the Six Months Ended 30 June 2026
Net rental income up 3.8% to €318m, distributable earnings per share up 3.5%, occupancy at 98.2%, and an S&P upgrade to BBB+ — NEPI Rockcastle's H1 2026 is the same quietly compounding story it always tells, and guidance was raised again.

NEPI Rockcastle's H1 2026 is another instalment of the same story: net rental and related income up 3.8% to €317.7m, distributable earnings per share up 3.5% to 32.14 euro cents, and a distribution declared at the same 3.5% growth rate (28.93 cents, a 90% payout ratio). Occupancy held at 98.2%, tenant sales rose 2.7% like-for-like with average basket size up 3.3%, and the collection rate reached 99% by end of July. Worth flagging: basic earnings per share grew a faster 7.3% to 42.01 cents, well ahead of the 2.1% growth in headline earnings per share (27.07 cents). That gap is explained by a €126m property revaluation gain, which flows through basic earnings but is excluded from headline earnings by definition — a normal feature of REIT accounting rather than a quality-of-earnings concern, but worth naming so the two numbers aren't confused.

The balance sheet continues to support the distribution rather than strain it. Loan-to-value ticked up slightly to 33.1% from 32.8% but remains comfortably below the company's 35% strategic ceiling, and S&P upgraded the credit rating to BBB+ in July 2026 (from BBB positive outlook), which should support cheaper refinancing as the development pipeline continues — the Promenada Bucharest extension and a renewable energy programme that already contributed €5.7m in H1, up 38% year-on-year. Management also confirmed NEPI Rockcastle's first investment outside Central and Eastern Europe, in Bilbao, Spain. That's a genuine strategic shift after nearly two decades of CEE-only concentration — a sensible diversification in principle, but it introduces execution and market-familiarity risk that the eighteen-year CEE track record doesn't cover, and is worth watching as the deal specifics emerge.

On valuation, the stock trades around 11-12x forward distributable earnings and roughly 1.1x book — not statistically cheap, but not demanding either, for a REIT compounding distributions at mid-single digits with near-full occupancy and an improving credit profile. Guidance was raised to 3.5-4% DEPS growth for the full year, up from the original February guidance. Rating: BULLISH. This is a pay-for-quality call rather than a bargain one — the risk to watch is CEE consumer spending momentum and how cleanly the Bilbao expansion is executed, not the balance sheet.

BEARISH Trustco Group Holdings (TTO) Review #1
18 Aug 2026
Requisitioned General Meeting — Results of Meeting
A shareholder-led attempt to remove Trustco's entire board, including founder Quinton van Rooyen, failed by roughly 58% to 42% — and was ruled invalid before the vote even happened, since the requisitionist wasn't a registered member. The governance drama is over; the underlying balance sheet stress isn't.

Trustco's requisitioned general meeting resolved every proposed resolution against the requisitionist: all resolutions to remove existing directors — including founder and controlling figure Quinton van Rooyen — failed by roughly 58% against to 42% in favour, and a proposed slate of five replacement directors was rejected by a similar margin (58.3% to 41.7%). The additional wrinkle is procedural: the meeting was found not to have been validly requisitioned in the first place, because the party who called it wasn't a registered member of the company. Voting was allowed to proceed anyway "for record purposes." The SENS announcement doesn't disclose who requisitioned the meeting or what specifically motivated the attempted board removal — an unusual gap in an otherwise significant governance filing, and one that limits how much can be inferred about the underlying dispute.

Set alongside the fundamentals, the governance outcome is the less important story. Trailing return on equity is -22.55%, debt-to-equity sits near 190%, and levered free cash flow is negative (around -R99m on a trailing basis). There's no meaningful trailing P/E because the company isn't consistently profitable. The share price itself is a red flag independent of any of this: it has shown essentially no active trading, sitting at 30 cents with a 52-week range (30 cents to R30.00) that looks more like a data or corporate-action artefact than genuine price discovery — itself a liquidity and transparency concern for anyone actually trying to hold or exit the stock.

For a value investor, a failed activist push removes near-term uncertainty about who sits on the board, but it resolves nothing about the business itself: no revenue or earnings detail was disclosed in this record, leverage is high, returns are negative, and the stock barely trades. Whatever prompted a shareholder to attempt an ouster wasn't explained here, which is itself a signal about the quality of information available to minority holders. Rating: BEARISH. There's no margin of safety in a loss-making, highly levered, thinly-traded stock, regardless of who wins the boardroom fight.

BULLISH BHP Group (BHG) Review #1
18 Aug 2026
FY2026 Results Announcement and Dividend Determination
Headline earnings per share up 31% to 239.1 US cents and the final dividend raised 65% to 99 US cents — but a $2.4bn impairment charge (versus just $154m a year earlier) means statutory basic EPS grew only 9%. The headline number is real, but it's flattering the underlying picture.

BHP's FY2026 headline earnings rose 31% to $12.14bn (239.1 US cents per share), on revenue up 15% to $58.76bn. That's a strong beat on its face, but the gap to statutory numbers matters: basic earnings per share grew only 9%, to 193.6 US cents, because FY2026 carried a $2.405bn impairment charge against property, plant, equipment and intangibles — versus just $154m in FY2025. Headline earnings are calculated to exclude impairments by design, which is exactly why the 31% headline growth figure overstates how much better the underlying, statutory year actually was. Notably, this isn't a one-off pattern for BHP: FY2024 also carried a large impairment ($3.9bn), meaning large individual write-downs have featured in each of the past three years' results — worth watching as a recurring feature of how this business reports, not a single year's noise. The short-form announcement doesn't identify which asset or segment drove this year's impairment; that detail sits in the full results PDF.

The dividend is the cleaner signal: a final dividend of 99 US cents, up 65% on the prior determination, and per the company's own disclosure the highest cash return to shareholders in several years. That's a genuine confidence signal from a management team roughly a year into a new CEO's tenure. Beyond the dividend and headline numbers, this short-form filing is thin — no segment breakdown, no balance sheet detail (debt, cash, gearing) is disclosed in the SENS text itself, which is a real limitation on how much a value investor can independently verify from this filing alone versus the full annual report.

On valuation, the stock isn't statistically cheap: trailing P/E of roughly 21.9x (still calculated on pre-results earnings and due to fall once refreshed), forward P/E around 17.3x, price-to-book near 4.4x, and EV/EBITDA around 8.2x, against a trailing ROE of about 24.7% and debt-to-equity near 52.6%. None of that is bargain territory for a cyclical miner — the market is already pricing in continued commodity strength. Rating: BULLISH on the strength of real (if impairment-obscured) earnings growth and a meaningfully larger dividend, but this is a quality-and-momentum call, not a margin-of-safety one; a commodity price pullback would hit a rich multiple harder than a cheap one.

BULLISH Absa Group (ABG) Review #1
18 Aug 2026
Unaudited Consolidated Interim Results and Ordinary Share Dividend Declaration for the Reporting Period Ended 30 June 2026
HEPS up 7.9% to 1,545.4 cents and the interim dividend up 8.3% to 850 cents, with the Stage 3 (bad loan) ratio improving from 5.90% to 5.30% even as net interest margin compressed. A dividend growing faster than earnings, on an improving credit book, close to book value.

Absa's interim results show headline earnings per share up 7.9% to 1,545.4 cents on total income growth of 4.1% to R58.79bn — earnings growing faster than revenue, with the cost-to-income ratio essentially flat at 53.4% (2025: 53.2%), meaning this isn't a cost-cutting story. Net interest margin compressed to 4.46% from 4.58% as rate cuts filter through the loan book, a genuine headwind that was more than offset by 5% growth in gross loans and 8% growth in deposits. Basic earnings per share grew faster still, up 12.1% to 1,517.9 cents — the reverse of the usual pattern, where headline earnings (which strip out one-off items) typically grow faster than the basic number. The short-form announcement doesn't itemise what's included in basic EPS but excluded from HEPS this period, so it's worth checking the full results for what specifically drove that gap rather than assuming it's automatically benign.

The more important number for a value investor is credit quality: the Stage 3 loans ratio — the closest proxy for bad debts — improved to 5.30% from 5.90% a year earlier. In a stretched South African consumer environment, an improving (not just stable) bad-loan ratio is a stronger signal than the headline earnings growth on its own, since credit deterioration is usually the first place trouble shows up at a bank. Capital position also strengthened: CET1 of 12.8% (2025: 12.5%) and a liquidity coverage ratio of 125.2% (down from 129.0% but still comfortably above the regulatory minimum) both point to spare capacity for further capital return. The interim dividend of 850 cents, up 8.3%, growing faster than HEPS, is a modest but real confidence signal from the board.

On valuation, the stock trades around 8.4x trailing earnings and roughly 1.0x book value, with a trailing return on equity near 13.7% (the company's own interim disclosure shows 15.0%). Trading close to book for a bank generating a mid-teens ROE isn't aggressively cheap once South Africa's elevated cost of equity is factored in, but it's not expensive either, and the 7.6%-plus dividend yield adds meaningfully to the total-return case. Rating: BULLISH. The standout here is credit quality moving in the right direction while margins compress — a harder combination to deliver than simple loan-book growth, and the more durable signal of the two.

BULLISH Thungela Resources (TGA) Review #1
17 Aug 2026
Interim Results for the Six Months Ended 30 June 2026 and R5.50 Interim Dividend Declaration
EPS up 467% to R10.95, adjusted operating free cash flow up 291% to R1.9bn, net cash R6.1bn, and a R5.50 interim dividend — nearly four times last year's payout. Full-year guidance reaffirmed. This is a debt-free coal exporter converting a cyclical price recovery straight into cash.

Thungela's H1 2026 numbers are a clean beat on its own 7 August trading statement, not just a good result in isolation. EPS of R10.95 and HEPS of R4.80 land within (and near the top of) the guided ranges. Revenue rose a modest 2% to R15.2bn — modest only because an 11% stronger average rand ate into what would otherwise have been a much larger increase, given benchmark coal prices ran 15% higher in South Africa and 25% higher in Australia. Adjusted EBITDA nearly doubled to R1.3bn. Export saleable production rose 6% to 8.5Mt, with Ensham in Australia the standout at 2.2Mt versus 1.6Mt a year ago, after finally working through the geological problems that hit H1 2025.

The balance sheet is the real story for a value investor. Total debt is essentially zero (R86m against R6.1bn of cash), debt-to-equity of 0.53% is about as close to a fortress balance sheet as a commodity miner gets, and the current ratio of 1.73 confirms ample liquidity. Adjusted operating free cash flow of R1.9bn — nearly four times the prior period, helped by R1.1bn from FX derivative gains — comfortably funds the R5.50 interim dividend (up 175% on last year's R2.00), a total cash distribution of R773m, while still leaving headroom for the Lephalale coal-bed-methane project and the pending Goedehoop North disposal. Management also flagged that South African environmental liabilities should be fully cash-collateralised by year-end once that disposal completes — a genuine de-risking of a liability line that has weighed on SA miners generically.

The caveat is the one that always applies to a thermal coal exporter: none of this is durable pricing power. Management itself frames the current price strength as linked to Middle East conflict-driven energy security concerns rather than a structural re-rating of coal demand, and flags downside risk from softening industrial demand and competing low-cost supply. But that's a reason to size the position for cyclicality, not a reason to avoid a company trading with a net cash balance sheet, reaffirmed guidance, and a management team that is visibly prioritising cash return over empire-building. Rating: BULLISH. At current levels this is a self-funding, debt-free cash compounder in a cyclical industry — the risk is the commodity cycle, not the balance sheet or the capital allocation.

BEARISH The SPAR Group (SPP) Review #1
17 Aug 2026
Resignation of Chairman and Deputy Chair, Appointment of Interim Chairman and Update on Board Succession
Chairman Mike Bosman and Deputy Chair Shirley Zinn both resign the same day — the third leadership upheaval since 2022. This lands on a balance sheet already carrying debt-to-equity above 300% and a current ratio under 1.0. Governance crisis meeting financial fragility is a bad combination.

The SENS announcement itself is carefully neutral — both directors "concluded that stepping down… is the right decision for them," the board "reaffirms its full and unqualified support" for the CEO and CFO, and "the Group's turnaround strategy and current guidance remain unaffected." Taken at face value, this reads like an orderly, voluntary transition. But context matters: Bosman was appointed Chairman in December 2022 specifically to stabilise the board after "serious governance matters" at the time, briefly served as Executive Chairman through October 2023, and is now the third senior leadership figure to depart since that governance crisis began (following a prior chairman and two changes of CEO). Zinn, who chaired remuneration and led work on malus/clawback policy, exits at the same time. Risk Committee chair Lwazi Koyana steps in as interim chairman while a permanent succession process runs, explicitly seeking directors with "direct retail, independent retailer and sector experience" — a specific and telling gap in the skills the board felt it needed.

That last detail is the signal worth weighing: SPAR's business model depends on independent retailers who own and operate the stores under the SPAR banner. A board that has just lost its chair and deputy chair, and is now recruiting explicitly for retailer relationship experience, suggests the underlying friction with that retailer base — the actual engine of SPAR's earnings — is unresolved, not a personnel matter that ends with two resignations. Repeated leadership churn at this frequency (three changes in under four years, now compounding into a double departure at the top of the board) is itself a governance red flag independent of what triggered any single exit.

The financial context makes this a worse time for a leadership vacuum than most. On trailing figures, SPAR carries total debt-to-equity above 300%, a current ratio of 0.99 (current liabilities exceed current assets), a thin trailing profit margin of -0.32%, and return on equity of just 5.76% — weak for a food retailer and well below what its debt load would need to generate an adequate return on borrowed capital. A business with that balance sheet structure needs stable, decisive leadership to manage supplier and lender relationships through a turnaround; instead it now has an interim chair and an unresolved retailer relationship to fix simultaneously. Rating: BEARISH. The board's insistence that strategy and guidance are "unaffected" is the kind of statement that's true right up until it isn't — watch for the permanent chair appointment and any commentary from the Spar Guild retailer body as the next real tests of whether this stabilises or deepens.

BEARISH RCL Foods (RCL) Review #1
17 Aug 2026
Trading Statement — Year Ended June 2026
HEPS guided down 30-35%, and underlying HEPS (which strips out impairments) is also down 25-30% — this is a genuine operating decline, not an accounting artefact. Sugar is being structurally undercut by untariffed imports, with no resolution date from ITAC.

RCL guides HEPS down 30-35% to 101.6-109.4c and EPS down 50-55% to 81-90.1c for the year to June 2026. The gap between the two is a 20.3c/share goodwill and brand impairment on the Sunshine cash-generating unit, triggered by volumes that never recovered from a December 2024 labour disruption at its Durban factory — a real, if isolated, operational failure being formally recognised now. But the more important number for a value investor is underlying HEPS, which strips out impairments and one-offs and is still guided down 25-30% to 102.3-109.6c. When the "clean" earnings measure falls by roughly the same magnitude as the headline measure, that tells you this isn't a case of one-off accounting noise flattering or distorting an otherwise sound operating year — the deterioration is real and operational.

The driver is almost entirely the Sugar segment and its associate, Royal Eswatini Sugar. With no effective tariff protection in place, deep-sea sugar imports flooded the local market: industry local-market volumes fell 10.3% while export volumes rose 48.3%, into a market where international raw sugar prices fell 22.6% and a stronger rand further compressed realised export proceeds. The statement puts a number on the damage — roughly R7,000/ton, or about 52%, gap between local and export sales prices. Critically, RCL says the tariff question remains unresolved with the International Trade Administration Commission "as at the date of this trading statement" — meaning this is an open-ended policy risk, not a problem with a known resolution date. Pet Food adds a second, separate wound: food-safety production disruptions (previously flagged in March) cut sales volumes 20.5% and drove higher stock write-offs. Culinary and Baking, by contrast, delivered "good performances," which is the one genuinely encouraging data point in the statement.

At R8.27, the stock trades on roughly 6.8x trailing earnings and 0.70x book value with a still-respectable trailing ROE of 10.7%, and the balance sheet isn't stressed — debt-to-equity of 22.4% and a current ratio of 1.63 leave room to absorb a bad year. That's the bull case: this looks statistically cheap. But cheap on a trailing multiple with underlying earnings guided down 25-30% and a structural, policy-dependent segment problem with no resolution timeline is not obviously undervalued — it's a business correctly priced for continued uncertainty. Rating: BEARISH. Full results land 31 August with segment detail; the ITAC tariff decision, whenever it comes, is the only catalyst that changes this thesis in either direction.

Rating: NEUTRAL. The direction of travel is positive and the size of the swing is real, but this is a speculative, thinly-traded small cap where the trading statement raises as many questions as it answers. NEUTRAL KAP Limited (KAP) Review #1

Updated Trading Statement for the Year Ended 30 June 2026
HEPS guided up 82–92% to 43.8–46.2c — but that's the flattering number. Unadjusted EPS swings to a loss (-6.2c to -3.8c) because three separate impairments (Restonic, Safripol, Optix) eat the gain. Real debt paydown is the genuine positive here.

KAP's headline earnings per share look like a turnaround story: guided to rise 82–92% to between 43.8c and 46.2c for FY26, versus 24.1c in FY25. That's driven by higher operating profit, lower net finance costs, and tax incentives tied to PG Bison's new medium-density fibreboard (MDF) line ramping up — genuine operating leverage, not accounting sleight of hand. Net debt was also cut by more than R1 billion, ahead of the company's own R500 million target, funded mostly by stronger cash generation. That's a real balance-sheet improvement worth crediting.

But HEPS is the flattering number in this statement. Unadjusted EPS is guided to swing from a small FY25 profit (0.4c) to a loss of between 3.8c and 6.2c in FY26. The gap is entirely impairments: goodwill on Restonic (the Sleep Group bedding acquisition) written down on weak domestic bedding demand; Safripol intangibles impaired because a stronger rand and depressed global polymer margins aren't expected to recover before 2030; and Optix intangibles impaired on continued underperformance in its Australian operations. Headline earnings measures exclude impairments by design — which is exactly why relying on HEPS alone here would miss that three separate acquisitions are being written down in a single trading statement.

That's the real signal for a value investor: this isn't one clean one-off — it's three unrelated units disappointing against the price KAP originally paid for them. That's a capital-allocation and quality-of-earnings flag, not just noise. At the same time, the underlying industrial businesses (PG Bison in particular) are genuinely improving, and the balance sheet is measurably stronger. Rating: NEUTRAL. Cheap-looking on a forward HEPS multiple, but wait for the 1 September full results and segment detail before treating the headline growth number at face value.

Loss per share "improved" — but only because heavy share dilution from a debt-for-equity recapitalisation spread a bigger loss over more shares. Revenue is flat. No path to profitability shown yet.

On the surface, Copper 360's headline loss per share improved 19.1% to -27.36c (from -33.82c) and basic loss per share improved 38.9% to -27.87c. That reads like progress. It isn't, in the way that matters most: revenue was essentially flat, down 0.4% to R143.2 million, while the actual loss after tax got worse, up 14.2% to R366.8 million. No dividend was declared, consistent with the prior year and unsurprising for a loss-making business.

For a value investor the core problem is unchanged: there's no revenue growth and no visible path to profitability in this announcement. Rating: BEARISH. The improved per-share loss is not evidence of a turnaround — it's evidence of dilution. Wait for clear signs of revenue growth or margin recovery before treating this as investable.
