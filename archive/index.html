Strong disclosed earnings momentum even after stripping once-offs, held inside a structure trading at an unusually wide discount to NAV; the 21 September full-year results are the next catalyst to confirm the quality of this growth, with RCL Foods/poultry-sector cyclicality the main investee-level risk to watch.

BULLISH City Lodge Hotels (CLH) Review #1
10 Sep 2026
Consolidated Annual Financial Statements and Cash Dividend Declaration for the year ended 30 June 2026
The 5% decline in statutory profit is a below-the-line story — an impairment of deferred tax assets and unrealised FX losses — not an operating one: adjusted HEPS, which strips those out, grew 20% to 41.6c, and management backed that with a R153m buyback that retired 6.4% of shares in issue, on top of a dividend that grew 27% to 19c for the year.

Revenue grew 10% to R2.2bn and adjusted EBITDAR grew 15% to R675m — margin expansion, not just volume growth. Group occupancy for the full year was 58% (up from 56%), though it peaked at 62% in the first half and softened into the second as higher fuel costs, inflation and interest rates weighed on discretionary domestic leisure travel; a 7% increase in average room rates (accelerating from 4% in H1 to nearly 10% in H2) more than offset the occupancy softness, which is the higher-quality way to grow revenue in a demand-constrained market.

The R153m share buyback — 38 million shares cancelled, reducing shares in issue by 6.4% — is the standout capital-allocation signal this desk missed on first pass: retiring stock while simultaneously pruning the portfolio (selling the loss-making Courtyard Arcadia, closing City Lodge Newtown) is exactly the kind of capital discipline a value investor wants to see. Cash generated from operations grew 20% to R657m, confirming the earnings are converting to real cash rather than sitting in receivables, and the group funded that buyback, R234m of refurbishment capex, and a growing dividend simultaneously — not the balance sheet profile of a business still stretched by the pandemic-era downturn.

Rating: BULLISH. At 11.3x trailing earnings and roughly 2x book this isn't statistically cheap, but the combination of real earnings growth (adjusted HEPS +20%), a buyback executed while the stock traded below where it sits today, and a dividend growing faster than earnings is a more complete recovery picture than the headline "profit fell 5%" figure suggests on its own. What would change the view: a second-half-style leisure travel slowdown extending into FY2027, the thing to watch at the next trading update.
