Investec Limited (INL) BULLISHReview #1
Friday, 18 Sep 2026 — Investec Group pre-close trading update
Guidance reaffirms May's targets with HEPS growth of 4–8% (38.1p–39.7p), driven by resilient Southern African returns (ROE 18.5–19.0%) offsetting a softer UK book — the growth is broad-based and backed by capital ratios still comfortably above regulatory minimums, not a story propped up by one segment.

Southern African adjusted operating profit is guided up to 6% ahead of the prior period in Rands (up to 14% in Pounds, flattered by Rand strength), with core loans up 10.3% on a reported basis and 6.3% on a neutral-currency basis — genuine book growth, not just currency translation. The UK business is guided 2–6% behind the prior period with a credit loss ratio drifting toward the top of its through-the-cycle range (35–55bps); the group blend of a strong SA engine carrying a moderating UK book is the real read here, not a standalone red flag.

Capital and asset quality remain sound: Investec Limited's CET1 ratio was 14.2% at 30 June and Investec plc's 12.7%, both comfortably above minima, with the group credit loss ratio guided within its 25–45bps target range. Converting a GBP-denominated earnings guide against a Rand share price makes a same-day self-calculated P/E unreliable, so this desk relies on third-party figures: trailing P/E of roughly 15.3x and P/B of roughly 2.1x. For a diversified bank and wealth manager targeting group ROE toward the upper end of 13–14% by FY2030, a low-to-mid-teens multiple is not demanding.

Rating: BULLISH. In-line-to-slightly-ahead guidance from a diversified franchise where Southern Africa is carrying the UK's softness is a durable, credible signal. What would change the view: a UK credit loss ratio breaking materially above its guided range, or a stall in Rathbones' funds-under-management momentum (up 13.8% since March), which is an increasingly important earnings contributor.

Alexander Forbes Group Holdings (AFH) BULLISHReview #1
Friday, 18 Sep 2026 — Proposed specific repurchase of shares and resultant directorate changes
A related-party buyback that retires 28.69% of shares in issue at a 3.4% discount to the 30-day VWAP, funded by a mix of cash and new debt, is exactly the kind of accretive capital action a value investor wants to see — though it materially increases leverage and further concentrates ownership under ARC AF Holdings.

Prudential Financial is exiting its remaining African emerging-markets exposure via New Veld, with ARC AF Holdings consolidating control (potentially up to 77.94% if a separate, related New Veld/ARC transaction also completes). This is fundamentally a controlling-shareholder transition, not an operating development — nothing here changes Alexforbes' underlying earnings trajectory. The value to remaining shareholders comes purely from the arithmetic: buying back stock below the prevailing market price and cancelling it is accretive to per-share metrics for whoever remains.

The transaction levers up the balance sheet meaningfully: roughly R2.1bn of new debt (a bridge facility at ZARONIA+2%, to be termed out), a ~R540m reduction in cash resources, and a R2.6bn reduction in share capital/reserves. That's a real change in the capital structure of a business that was presumably conservatively financed before, and the roughly R145m/year of incremental finance charges is a permanent claim on future earnings that didn't exist last week.

On valuation, third-party trailing P/E estimates ranged from roughly 12x to 18x this week (dispersed, so treated with some caution) against a disclosed ROE near 19.3% — a decent-quality financial services franchise (retirement, employee benefits consulting, multi-manager investments) at a reasonable multiple even before accounting for the accretion from the buyback itself.

Rating: BULLISH. The discount-to-VWAP pricing and resulting EPS accretion for remaining shareholders is the clear positive; the risks worth tracking are execution and leverage — specifically the terms of the long-term funding that replaces the bridge facility — and the governance implications of ARC AF Holdings' growing concentration, rather than anything questioning the operating business.

Selling its only Baltic asset at a 13% premium to its own IFRS net asset value, with net proceeds earmarked for the core CEE platform and the newly entered Spanish market, is capital discipline in action — the kind of portfolio recycling that validates rather than undermines the bullish thesis from three weeks ago.

The transaction is explicitly stated as not categorisable under the JSE Listings Requirements relative to NEPI Rockcastle's roughly €8.4bn portfolio — small in absolute size, but the signal matters more than the rand amount: selling a well-performing, fully-let asset well above carrying value, then redeploying into higher-growth CEE markets and Spain, is disciplined capital allocation rather than a distressed or reactive disposal.

Net proceeds of €179m are a modest addition to an already well-capitalised REIT, but the strategic logic — concentrating capital in markets where NEPI Rockcastle can achieve dominant, scaled positions rather than maintaining a single outlying asset in a region it no longer wants exposure to — is coherent and consistent with the disciplined capital-recycling behaviour this desk flagged favourably on 18 August.

On valuation, third-party figures put NRP at roughly 10.6x trailing P/E (JSE), 1.15x price-to-book, and around 13.8x EV/EBITDA — not statistically cheap for a REIT, but NEPI Rockcastle has consistently commanded a premium to SA-focused property peers given its CEE growth profile and a demonstrated track record of value-accretive transactions like this one.

Rating: BULLISH, unchanged and reinforced from the 18 August call. A premium-to-NAV exit from a non-core market is exactly the capital discipline this desk previously cited as supportive of the thesis. What to watch: execution and initial performance of the new Spanish investment as the next test of whether redeployed capital earns an adequate return.

Choppies Enterprises (CHP) BEARISHReview #1
Friday, 18 Sep 2026 — Audited Group financial results for the year ended 30 June 2026
Revenue growing 7.8% while operating profit falls 23.6% and headline earnings per share nearly halve is a textbook margin-compression story, and management's decision to suspend the dividend entirely — rather than merely trim it — signals this is viewed internally as more than a one-year blip.

The gap between top-line growth (+7.8% to BWP9,889m) and HEPS (-56.4% continuing operations to 4.1 Thebe, -49% on a total-operations basis) is driven by genuine, disclosed macro headwinds rather than a one-off accounting item: reduced consumer liquidity from Botswana's diamond-market slump, Pula devaluation, government austerity measures, an oil-price-driven rise in fuel and transport costs, a newly implemented living wage, and like-for-like sales that actually declined 1.2% even as new stores added volume. That combination is arguably a worse signal than a one-off charge would be, because it points to structural, economy-wide cost pressure that doesn't reverse on its own.

Management's own decision not to declare a final dividend (2025: 0.6 thebe) "to preserve liquidity for core operations" is the clearest possible confirmation that this isn't being treated as a temporary dip; net cash generated from operating activities fell from BWP679m to BWP578m over the year, and the effective tax rate rose to 43.5% (from 30.7%) due to unrecognised deferred tax on Namibian losses, compounding the profit decline further down the income statement.

Valuation multiples for CHP were unusually unreliable this week: reported trailing P/E figures ranged from roughly 8x to 19x, and because Choppies' primary listing (and its HEPS, disclosed in Botswana Thebe) sits on the BSE while the JSE line trades in Rand, a same-day self-calculated multiple would require a Pula/Rand conversion this desk did not attempt with confidence. No single multiple is relied upon above.

Rating: BEARISH. Margin destruction this broad-based — hitting nearly every disclosed cost line simultaneously — combined with a suspended dividend as management's own confirmation, argues for staying away until there is evidence the Botswana consumer and cost backdrop has stabilised.

Mpact (MPT) NEUTRALReview #1
Friday, 18 Sep 2026 — Category 2 transaction announcement relating to the disposal of the Versapak Paarl property
A R185m disposal of a non-core Paarl property at more than double its book value is sensible portfolio housekeeping and debt paydown, but it's immaterial next to Mpact's roughly R14bn revenue base and doesn't move the investment case either way.

The property — formerly occupied by Mpact's own Versapak division, now leased to Sinica Versapak on a market-related lease to 2029 — generated only R17.5–19.6m of annual rental income against a R89.2m net book value. Selling it as a going concern for R185m, more than double book value, and applying the proceeds to existing debt is textbook non-core asset recycling: crystallising a valuation gain on an asset that was earning a low yield relative to its worth to Mpact specifically.

Applying the proceeds directly to debt settlement is the right capital-allocation call given Mpact's leverage profile (third-party estimates put net debt near R2.5bn and debt-to-equity near 0.54x); every non-core rand redeployed this way modestly de-risks the balance sheet without diluting shareholders or requiring new financing.

On valuation, third-party figures show Mpact trading at roughly 6.4x trailing earnings and 3.96x EV/EBITDA — statistically cheap on a headline basis for a diversified packaging business, though that multiple has arguably reflected sector overcapacity and cyclical paper/packaging pricing pressure for some time; nothing in this specific property disposal changes that broader industry read one way or the other.

Rating: NEUTRAL. A well-executed but immaterial transaction relative to group scale. The cheap headline multiple is a genuine standing value signal on its own, but this specific disposal doesn't provide enough new information to move the rating without more visibility on group-level packaging demand and pricing, expected at the next results update.

SA Corporate Real Estate (SAC) BULLISHReview #1
Thursday, 17 Sep 2026 — Unaudited condensed consolidated interim financial results for the six months ended 30 June 2026 and cash dividend declaration
Distribution per share growing 7% and HEPS growing 37% in the same half that the traditional portfolio's vacancy rate ticked up (1.5% to 1.8%) is a reminder to look past the headline growth numbers, but the underlying combination of net property income growth, an active R1.7bn disposal programme, and a payout ratio held disciplined at 92.5% is a genuinely solid REIT result.

HEPS growth (+37% to 18.32c) meaningfully outpaced distributable income growth (+7% to 15.05c) — a normal REIT divergence, since headline/basic earnings capture fair-value property revaluations that don't flow into the cash distribution. That makes the more conservative distribution-per-share number (13.92c, +7%) the better read on cash actually available to shareholders, and 7% is still comfortably ahead of SA CPI.

Like-for-like net property income grew 5.5% to R655.1m and operating profit grew 11.8% to R796.5m — genuine organic growth in the core portfolio rather than growth manufactured through revaluation or acquisition alone. The R1,731.0m of property disposals transferred, contracted or still to transfer over the six months is funding this growth and portfolio quality improvement without obviously stretching the balance sheet; separately, the board also extended the CEO's employment contract and made board committee changes in the same week, a modest continuity signal alongside the results.

On valuation, a same-day self-calculated trailing P/E (today's price ÷ annualised H1 HEPS of 36.64c) comes to roughly 9.4x, and third-party sources put price-to-book near 0.69x with ROE near 11.3%. Trading meaningfully below net asset value while delivering high-single-digit distribution growth is an attractive combination for a diversified (residential, industrial, retail) REIT.

Rating: BULLISH. Real distribution growth, a sub-1x P/B, and an active capital-recycling programme funding growth without visible leverage stress outweigh the modest uptick in traditional-portfolio vacancy. What would change the view: vacancy continuing to climb at the full-year mark, which would suggest a genuine leasing softness cycle rather than a one-off blip.

An unprompted guidance upgrade — full-year Gross Profit growth now expected in the low-to-mid teens — on the back of 19% invoiced-income growth is about as clean a beat-and-raise as a trading update gets, even though Operating Profit growth of only around 6% shows cost normalisation is genuinely biting into the flow-through.

Net cash of approximately £68m after returning £41.3m to shareholders (a £16.3m final dividend plus £25.0m of buybacks) in the same half is the standout capital-discipline signal: the group is growing invoiced income at 19%, converting cash at a normal (if seasonally lighter first-half) rate of around 45%, and returning capital simultaneously — the profile of a well-run operator rather than one straining to fund growth.

On valuation, the LSE-listed primary metric puts BYI at roughly 18.8x trailing earnings — not a statistically cheap multiple, but for a business compounding gross profit in the mid-teens, with AI increasingly cited by management as a growth driver across its core cloud, security and software categories, and a demonstrated track record of returning cash to shareholders, the premium looks more earned than speculative.

Rating: BULLISH. A genuine guidance upgrade backed by strong top-line momentum and continued capital discipline is a clean positive signal. York Timber Holdings (YRK) NEUTRALReview #1
Thursday, 17 Sep 2026 — Trading statement and trading update, including notification of the unbundling of certain assets
A trading statement guiding total HEPS down 64–69% looks alarming, but the entire decline is explained by the accounting reclassification of two units (Stadsrivier, Mbulwa) as discontinued ahead of an unbundling — continuing-operations cash generation is actually up 25–30%, a materially better underlying picture than the headline collapse suggests.

Continuing-operations EPS is guided down "only" 54–59% — still a genuine decline — while total-operations HEPS falls 64–69% purely because of the IFRS 5 discontinued-operations treatment of the units being unbundled to shareholders. Separating the underlying continuing-business trend from the accounting-driven total is essential to reading this correctly, and York's own disclosure, unusually clearly laid out across continuing, discontinued and total bases, makes that separation possible.

Cash generated from continuing operations is guided up 25–30% to R182–189m despite the earnings decline, and EBITDA from continuing operations is roughly flat to slightly up (+1–4%) — a business generating more cash while reported earnings fall is a genuinely different, and better, signal than earnings and cash deteriorating together. Core EPS (stripping biological-asset fair-value movements) is guided to a small loss, a reminder that forestry earnings carry an inherent non-cash valuation swing on top of the operating result.

A same-day self-calculated forward P/E on the new, lower guided HEPS base comes to roughly 10x, versus a stale third-party trailing multiple near 3.8x still reflecting last year's much higher earnings base — the re-rating on a like-for-like guided-earnings basis is real, and the stock is no longer statistically as cheap as the historical multiple alone would suggest.

Rating: NEUTRAL. Cash-generation resilience through the earnings decline is a genuine positive, and the unbundling could eventually surface value in a cleaner forestry-pure-play structure, but earnings visibility remains muddied until the full FY results (due on or about 30 September) and the final terms of the unbundling are known — not yet a clear enough setup to call bullish or bearish.

Thursday, 17 Sep 2026 — Dividend declaration, availability of capitalisation issue circular and finalisation information in respect of the capitalisation issue — ← Previous: NEUTRAL on 3 Sep 2026 (audited annual results)
This is a procedural follow-through on the FY26 results reported two weeks ago — shareholders choosing between a 90.91c cash dividend or scrip via a capitalisation issue — rather than new fundamental information, so the prior Neutral call stands unchanged.

There is no new operating disclosure in this announcement; the capitalisation-issue mechanics (a ratio of 3.78792 new shares per 100 held, priced at a R24.00 reference price) simply give shareholders optionality between taking cash or reinvesting via scrip, funded either way from income reserves rather than by raising new external capital — a reasonable, low-cost way to let the market self-select between liquidity and compounding exposure.

Funding the election from income reserves rather than new debt or an accelerated bookbuild is a mild structural positive relative to some REIT capital-raise mechanisms, though it doesn't itself change portfolio quality, leverage, or the growth outlook one way or the other.

Rating: NEUTRAL, unchanged from 3 September. Nothing in this specific announcement moves the fundamental picture one way or the other; the cash-versus-scrip election is a personal liquidity and reinvestment decision for shareholders rather than a signal about the underlying business.
