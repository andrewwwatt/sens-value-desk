Netcare (NTC) BULLISHReview #1
Thursday, 1 Oct 2026 — Voluntary update on FY2026 trading
Resilient mid-single-digit revenue growth, an 8.2% jump in mental-health patient days, and a R1.042bn buyback completed during the year is a defensive healthcare operator compounding steadily and returning capital, at a reasonable mid-teens multiple.

Total paid patient days growth of only 1.6% looks modest on its own, but group revenue guided up 4.0% (hospital and emergency services revenue up 4.2%) shows pricing and case-mix are doing useful work alongside volumes, and the 8.2% growth in mental-health patient days points to a genuine structural growth pocket within the portfolio rather than reliance on the core acute business alone.

Repurchasing 60.2 million shares (R1.042bn, average 1,724c) during the year is a meaningful capital return relative to Netcare's market cap, and doing so while still guiding positive revenue growth suggests management sees the buyback as value-accretive rather than a signal of limited growth opportunities elsewhere. A trailing P/E near 12.7x, P/B near 2.0x and ROE around 16% is a reasonable, not demanding, rating for a private-hospital operator with resilient demand.

Rating: BULLISH. Steady low-single-digit volume growth, a structurally growing mental-health segment, and consistent buybacks at a mid-teens multiple together support a constructive view; full FY2026 results on or about 23 November will be the next test of whether the guided 4% revenue growth holds through to the bottom line.

Mustek (MST) BEARISHReview #1
Thursday, 1 Oct 2026 — Short form announcement: Financial results for the year ended 30 June 2026 and cash dividend declaration
Headline earnings per share up 181% looks spectacular until the source is examined: lower finance costs, FX gains and higher associate income did the work while gross profit actually fell 3.4%, and operating cash generation collapsed from R687.4m to R115.8m — enough that the new, higher dividend now costs more than operations generated after interest and tax.

Revenue grew just 1% to R7.26bn while gross margin contracted to 12.7% from 13.3% and gross profit fell 3.4% — the core IT-distribution trading business is not where this year's earnings came from. The 181% HEPS increase to 204.6c is explicitly attributed to lower finance costs, foreign exchange gains and higher profit from associates, none of which reflect improved trading conditions in the underlying distribution business.

The dividend decision is the clearest red flag: the board more than doubled the final dividend to 37.5c (from 13.75c) in the same year that cash generated from operations fell from R687.4m to R115.8m, and the dividend cost (~R20m) now exceeds the roughly R15.6m operations generated after interest and tax. Raising the payout against a backdrop of collapsing operating cash generation is the kind of decision that borrows from balance-sheet strength rather than reflects current earnings power.

Rating: BEARISH. A statistically cheap P/E of ~7.6x and P/B of ~0.51x might ordinarily look attractive, but the earnings quality here is poor — non-operating items drove the entire HEPS beat while core gross profit declined, and a dividend increase funded ahead of operating cash generation adds a capital-discipline concern on top of the margin pressure.

Last price: ~R15.99 (aggregator)  ·  FY2026 HEPS: 204.6c (+181%, non-operating driven)  ·  Gross profit: -3.4%  ·  Cash from operations: R115.8m (down from R687.4m)  ·  Final dividend: 37.5c (from 13.75c)  ·  Trailing P/E: ~7.6x  ·  P/B: ~0.51x
The statistically cheap trailing P/E and P/B above should be read alongside this week's disclosure that the HEPS beat was driven by non-operating items, not multiplied blindly as a value signal.

Capitec Bank Holdings (CPI) NEUTRALReview #2
Wednesday, 30 Sep 2026 — Unaudited condensed consolidated financial results and ordinary cash dividend declaration for the six months ended 31 August 2026 ← Previous: NEUTRAL on 10 Sep 2026 (trading statement)
Record headline earnings per share growth of 19% to 8,262c on operating profit up 21% is an unambiguously strong half, but a stock already trading near 30x trailing earnings and roughly 8.6x book value is pricing in continued perfection, leaving little room for a growth hiccup.

Operating profit before tax growth of 21% to R12.632bn outpacing even HEPS growth (19%) is the right order of operating leverage — profitability is genuinely improving rather than being flattered by below-the-line items. The 19% increase in the interim dividend to 3,110c tracks earnings growth almost exactly, a sign management isn't stretching the payout ahead of underlying cash generation, and client numbers continuing past 26.6 million shows the growth is volume-driven, not just pricing.

But a trailing P/E near 29.6x and P/B near 8.6x leave almost no margin of safety: any deceleration in loan growth, a turn in the credit cycle, or adverse regulatory change would compress a multiple this rich quickly, and the market is already assuming years of continued high-teens growth.

Rating: NEUTRAL. The operating performance alone would support a bullish call on fundamentals, but at these multiples the stock already prices in sustained 19%+ growth — this desk prefers to acknowledge quality without chasing a valuation that leaves no cushion for disappointment.

MC Mining (MCZ) BEARISHReview #1
Wednesday, 30 Sep 2026 — Results for the full year ended 30 June 2026
A 51% narrower loss sounds like progress until the cause is read: revenue collapsed 57% after the Uitkomst mine stopped production in March, and cash on hand nearly halved to US$2.9m — a shrinking-business story dressed up as an improving-loss story.

Revenue falling to $7.4m from $17.5m, with cost of sales down a smaller 35% to $15.6m, actually produced a wider gross loss ($8.1m vs $6.6m the prior year) — the narrower bottom-line loss ($17.8m vs $36.0m) is explained almost entirely by the absence of a FY2025 impairment charge ($24.3m that year, nil this year), not by any operating improvement. Strip out that one-off and the underlying trend is negative.

US$2.9m of cash (down from $7.4m) against rising administrative expenses (+42% to $9.8m) is a thin runway for a company that just lost its primary producing asset. The market appears to be pricing a speculative recovery story — the shares have risen sharply over the past year — well ahead of any visible path back to production or profitability.

Rating: BEARISH. A shrinking revenue base, a widening gross loss, a thinning cash position and a share price that has already run hard on speculation rather than delivered results together argue for caution until Uitkomst restarts or an alternative revenue source is confirmed.

Heriot REIT (HET) BULLISHReview #1
Wednesday, 30 Sep 2026 — Results for the year ended 30 June 2026 and final dividend declaration
Guidance raised mid-year to 14–17% dividend-per-share growth (from 10–15%), on top of 16.3% distributable earnings growth already booked at the interim stage, is a REIT compounding distributions at a rate most of its JSE peers aren't matching, at a still-undemanding multiple.

A 100%-distribution policy means the dividend growth rate is effectively the earnings growth rate here — there is little room for the kind of REIT accounting noise (revaluations, once-off fair-value gains) that can flatter headline earnings without a cash backing. Management raising full-year dividend-growth guidance mid-stream, rather than merely reaffirming it, is a credible signal that the interim momentum carried through rather than faded.

A trailing P/E near 6.4x and P/B near 1.1x is not an expensive entry point for double-digit distribution growth, particularly against a sector where several larger, lower-growth REIT peers trade at similar or higher multiples. The upgrade itself, occurring without an offsetting disclosure of rising vacancies or credit losses, suggests the growth is organic rather than financially engineered.

Rating: BULLISH. Raised guidance, a 100%-payout discipline, and a reasonable multiple together make this an attractive small-cap REIT; the main watch-item for the next review is whether the full detail behind the final FY2026 distribution growth figure, once published in full, confirms the guided range was met or exceeded.

MAS plc (MSP) BULLISHReview #1
Tuesday, 29 Sep 2026 — Short-form announcement: Audited consolidated financial results for the year to 30 June 2026
A 5% total shareholder return for the year is unremarkable on its own, but repurchasing 28.5 million shares on-market at €1.0726 while the stock trades at roughly 0.6x of its own disclosed NAV is the more important fact — management buying back stock at a steep discount to net asset value is a clear, repeatable value signal.

The Development Joint Venture's 47.8% stake (via PK Investments) shows a controlling shareholder with its own capital committed alongside minority holders, which aligns incentives around NAV realisation rather than short-term earnings optics. Returns are generated from directly-owned Central and Eastern European income property, other investments including the Development JV, and corporate activities — a diversified property-and-investment structure rather than a single-asset bet.

A P/B near 0.63x and Price/NAV near 0.61x mean the market is pricing MAS at roughly 60c for every euro of disclosed net asset value — buybacks executed at or near that discount are mechanically accretive to remaining shareholders' per-share NAV, which is the clearest form of capital discipline available to a property company trading below book.

Rating: BULLISH. A persistent, wide discount to NAV combined with an active, discount-accretive buyback programme is a textbook value setup; the main risk is further NAV erosion in the underlying CEE property portfolio outpacing the rate of buyback-driven accretion.

Accelerate Property Fund (APF) NEUTRALReview #1
Tuesday, 29 Sep 2026 — Regularisation of the Fourways Mall management agreement and potential minority stake disposal to the managers
Vacancies at Fourways Mall falling from 18.8% to 6.6% and trading density up nearly 50% under the current managers is a genuine, substantial operational turnaround at Accelerate's flagship asset, but a debt-to-equity ratio above 100% and a razor-thin cash position mean the balance sheet, not the mall, is still the dominant risk in this stock.

The new management agreement with Flanagan & Gerard Frontiers and Luvon Investments formalises an arrangement that has demonstrably worked: tenant turnover rising from R226.3m to R365.2m and trading density from R1,816/m² to R2,711/m² are hard, verifiable performance improvements at South Africa's largest shopping centre, not soft guidance. The option for managers to earn up to a 15% stake as an upside participation fee aligns their incentives with continued performance, though it is also a real future dilution of Accelerate and Azrapart's economic interest in the asset if targets are met.

None of this changes Accelerate's balance sheet reality: debt-to-equity above 100% and debt of roughly R3.7bn against minimal cash (~R13.6m) is a genuinely stretched capital structure for a REIT, and a successful Fourways Mall turnaround primarily benefits debt serviceability and asset value rather than near-term distributable income given the group's broader leverage constraints.

Rating: NEUTRAL. The operational improvement at Fourways Mall is real and well-evidenced, but it is happening inside a balance sheet that remains the dominant constraint on the equity story; this desk would need to see visible deleveraging, not just asset-level performance, before turning constructive.

PPC (PPC) NEUTRALReview #1
Monday, 28 Sep 2026 — Operating update for the five months ended 31 August 2026
Group EBITDA up 40% against flat revenue is a genuinely strong margin outcome, but it is happening while South African and Botswana cement revenue actually declined 2% on continued competitor discounting — the improvement is more cost and mix discipline than a demand recovery in PPC's core market.

Zimbabwe's 5% revenue growth is the one segment showing genuine volume momentum; South Africa and Botswana cement revenue falling 2% despite price and product-mix improvements confirms management's own admission that it does not anticipate a near-term improvement in SA cement trading conditions while competitors continue discounting. A 40% EBITDA increase on a roughly flat top line is therefore a cost-and-pricing-discipline story, not a volume one.

Balance sheet metrics are comfortably conservative (debt-to-equity near 0.12x, cash-to-debt near 1.23x), which matters given PPC is mid-construction on its new integrated RK3 cement plant in the Western Cape — a low-leverage starting position gives room to fund that capex without strain. A trailing P/E near 13.5x and EV/EBITDA near 5.1x (slightly above its 3-year average of 4.6x) is a fair, not cheap, rating given the subdued SA volume backdrop.

Rating: NEUTRAL. Margin discipline and a strong balance sheet are genuine positives, but with management itself flagging no near-term SA demand recovery and competitor discounting persisting, this isn't yet a setup that justifies paying a premium to the stock's own historical multiple; the RK3 plant completion is the next catalyst worth watching.
