Santam (SNT) BULLISHReview #2
Monday, 5 Oct 2026 — Joint Firm Intention Announcement: Sanlam offer to acquire all Santam shares not already owned via Scheme of Arrangement ← Previous: BULLISH on 3 Sep 2026 (interim results + dividend)
Sanlam's R505/share all-cash offer — a 26.6% premium to Santam's last close and above its prior all-time high — converts Santam from a standalone compounding story into a merger-arbitrage situation almost certain to complete, given Sanlam already controls 62.7% and Santam's independent board has unanimously recommended the deal.

Unlike a trading update, there is no new earnings disclosure here — the relevant number is the price. At R505, Sanlam is paying roughly 25% above the 30-day VWAP and 28.6% above the 90-day VWAP, materially above the R475 the stock closed at on the news itself (up 19% intraday from R399.75). The roughly 6% gap between the offer price and the post-announcement trading price is the market's own estimate of completion risk and timing — shareholder vote, regulatory approvals, scheme sanction — not a signal the deal is in doubt.

Before this announcement Santam was already a high-quality underwriter: ROE near 32% against a 24% internal target, and a price-to-NAV of roughly 3.1x–3.4x that had been drifting toward, not exceeding, its pre-COVID 3.2x–3.7x trading range. An offer at R505 implies a further step-up in that multiple — Sanlam is paying up for certainty of full ownership of a scarce, high-ROE short-term insurer rather than waiting for the market to re-rate it on its own.

Rating: BULLISH. With Sanlam's board, Santam's independent board and the existing 62.7% stake all aligned behind R505, the probability-weighted return from holding into the scheme — locking in the current ~6% spread to the offer price over a matter of months — looks attractive relative to the risk, though this is now a completion/timing call rather than a traditional earnings-growth thesis; a vote delay, a competing higher bid (one analyst has suggested fair value closer to R550), or a regulatory snag would be the catalysts to watch.

Sanlam (SLM) NEUTRALReview #2
Monday, 5 Oct 2026 — Joint Firm Intention Announcement: offer to acquire remaining Santam shares via Scheme of Arrangement ← Previous: NEUTRAL on 10 Sep 2026 (interim results)
Paying a 26.6% premium — above Santam's own all-time high — to buy out the roughly 37% of Santam it doesn't already own is a large, high-conviction capital commitment that converts a volatile minority-interest earnings line into full, certain ownership of one of the group's best-performing franchises, but it is capital that could otherwise have funded buybacks in Sanlam's own, more modestly rated shares.

Full ownership of Santam removes the minority-interest drag that has periodically muddied Sanlam's consolidated numbers, and locks in exposure to a business earning a ~32% ROE — comfortably ahead of what Sanlam itself generates (estimates cluster around 20% on a trailing basis). Strategically this is a clean, understandable move: pay up now for permanent full exposure to the group's highest-returning unit rather than share those returns with outside shareholders indefinitely.

The price paid is not cheap by any measure quoted in the market — 25%–28.6% above Santam's recent VWAP, above its prior all-time high — and funding details (cash on balance sheet versus new debt) were not disclosed in what this review could access. Sanlam's own valuation (trailing P/E estimates ranging roughly 8.7x–12.9x and P/B around 1.7x–1.9x depending on source and date, too dispersed to anchor on a single figure) gives no strong read on whether the market is rewarding or merely absorbing this capital deployment yet.

Rating: NEUTRAL. The strategic logic — full ownership of a 32%-ROE insurer — is sound, but paying a rich, above-all-time-high premium for an asset already majority-owned is a lower-margin-of-safety use of capital than buying back Sanlam's own shares at a single-digit-to-low-teens P/E; this is a "wait for the numbers" call until funding terms and the pro forma earnings impact are disclosed.

Woolworths Holdings (WHL) NEUTRALReview #2
Wednesday, 8 Oct 2026 — Voluntary announcement relating to the acquisition of 100% of the issued shares of in2food Holdings Proprietary Limited ← Previous: NEUTRAL on 2 Sep 2026 (annual results + dividend)
Buying full ownership of in2food — a R5bn-revenue prepared-foods supplier that is already Woolworths Foods' most significant vendor — is a sensible vertical-integration move that secures a critical supply relationship, but with no purchase price, funding structure or earnings-accretion detail disclosed in this announcement, there isn't yet enough to move the rating off where last month's results left it.

The strategic rationale is straightforward: in2food's revenue exceeds R5bn a year and Woolworths Foods is already its largest customer, so bringing the supplier in-house removes a layer of margin leakage and gives Woolworths direct control over a key input to its higher-margin prepared/convenience-food category, one of the more defensible parts of SA grocery retail. Management's stated intent to run in2food as a standalone business and preserve its entrepreneurial culture suggests this is being treated as a genuine capability acquisition, not just a cost play.

What's missing is the number that matters for a value call: the purchase price. One report characterises the deal as expected to be earnings-accretive, but without the consideration, funding mix or the multiple paid, it isn't possible to assess whether this is value-creating at the price actually paid. The deal also remains subject to Competition Commission clearance, adding execution/timing risk on top of the valuation unknown. At the group level, WHL's own multiple (trailing P/E recently around 20x, P/B near 4.6x, ROE estimates ranging 25%–31% depending on period) already prices in a reasonably optimistic outlook.

Rating: NEUTRAL, unchanged from 2 September. The acquisition itself is a logical, strategically low-risk move, but the lack of price and accretion detail means this desk can't yet upgrade the view; the next catalyst is Competition Commission clearance and, ideally, disclosure of the consideration paid.

Afrimat (AFT) BEARISHReview #1
Tuesday, 7 Oct 2026 — Trading statement for the six-month period ended 31 August 2026
A swing from HEPS of 101.9c a year ago to a guided headline loss of 55c–60c this half — in what management itself calls the toughest trading conditions since Afrimat's 2006 listing — is a genuine operating deterioration, not an accounting artefact, and the stock should be priced for a cyclical trough rather than a one-off blip.

The gap between the headline loss (55c–60c) and basic EPS (guided to just 0.1c–5.2c, near break-even) is explained by gains on Competition Commission-mandated divestitures and non-core asset disposals, which are excluded from headline earnings by definition — so the less-bad EPS number is itself a one-off, not evidence the core business is close to profitable. Both measures point to a severely pressured half.

Management attributes the deterioration to a stronger rand (hurting export competitiveness) and a 49.1% spike in shipping costs linked to the Iran conflict, which together pushed average mine-gate revenue per tonne down 16.4%; domestic iron ore volumes remained roughly 36.5% below the comparative period. These are largely external, cyclical/macro pressures on the export-facing iron-ore business rather than a loss of market share or structural decline in Afrimat's broader, diversified construction-materials base, which matters for how durable the damage is.

Rating: BEARISH. A trailing P/E in the low-to-mid 30s and a price-to-NAV near 1.2x–1.33x (per pre-announcement snapshots, now moot given the loss) were already not obviously cheap heading into a guided headline loss; with no clear near-term catalyst for rand weakness or shipping-cost relief, this is a name to watch for stabilisation in iron-ore export economics before revisiting, rather than a buy-the-dip call today.

H1 FY27 HEPS: guided loss of 55c–60c (vs +101.9c PY)  ·  Basic EPS: 0.1c–5.2c (-95% to -100%)  ·  Mine-gate revenue/tonne: -16.4%  ·  Shipping costs: +49.1%  ·  Domestic iron ore volumes: -36.5% vs PY

OUTsurance Group (OUT) NEUTRALReview #2
Tuesday, 6 Oct 2026 — Acquisition of the remaining shares in Outsurance Holdings Limited from the OHL minority shareholders ← Previous: NEUTRAL on 10 Sep 2026 (results + ordinary and special dividends)
Buying out the roughly 7.17% of OUTsurance Holdings still held by directors, management and employees — in an all-share deal valued at about R10.2bn — is a sensible simplification of group structure, but because the sellers are the company's own executives and the final exchange ratio is still floating against a 30-day VWAP, this is a related-party transaction whose fairness depends on mechanics not yet finalised.

Paying in OGL shares rather than cash avoids a cash outflow, and folding in the last sliver of minority ownership at the OHL subsidiary level removes a structural complication — minority-interest accounting, potential conflicts between OHL management-shareholders and the listed group — ahead of a cleaner, fully-owned structure going forward. The JSE's related-party classification (sellers include group CEO Marthinus Visser and other executives) is the right regulatory lens, and setting the exchange ratio mechanically off a 30-day VWAP window (6 Oct–17 Nov) rather than negotiating it is a reasonable safeguard against insider-favourable pricing.

OUTsurance's own multiple is rich by any reading found: a forward P/E cited around 20x (against roughly 12x for close peer Santam), a price-to-NAV as high as 7.5x in one source, and ROE estimates clustering 29%–33%. High ROE partly justifies a premium rating, but 7.5x book is a demanding multiple for a short-term insurer, leaving limited margin of safety if growth or underwriting results disappoint.

Rating: NEUTRAL. The transaction itself is value-neutral in substance — converting a minority stake into more OGL shares at a market-determined ratio — and reasonably governed, but it doesn't change the underlying question of whether OUTsurance's own 7x-plus book multiple is sustainable; that remains the swing factor, not this housekeeping transaction.

Sappi (SAP) NEUTRALReview #1
Monday, 5 Oct 2026 — Update on Proposed Graphic Papers Joint Venture with UPM
Combining Sappi's European graphic-paper mills with UPM's Communication Papers business into a 50/50 joint venture, targeting roughly €100m of annual synergies, is a rational response to a structurally shrinking end-market — but Sappi's own recent profitability has been weak-to-negative, so the JV's value depends heavily on execution of those synergies rather than the state of the standalone business today.

The contributed-asset split (UPM's business valued at roughly €1,100m, Sappi's at roughly €320m, with €600m of external financing and a €100m revolving facility secured and underwritten by Citi and Nordea) suggests this has moved well past a letter-of-intent into a financed, structured transaction, with final resolutions targeted by end-2026. Combining declining graphic-paper volumes under one larger, better-utilised asset base is the standard playbook for managing a secularly shrinking category, and the stated €100m synergy target, if delivered, would be meaningful relative to the scale of assets contributed.

Sappi's own recent fundamentals are genuinely difficult to anchor: search results show a trailing P/E as high as ~24.7x in one snapshot but negative earnings (hence no P/E) in another dated around the same period, alongside a price-to-book that has ranged from under 1x to over 6x across different dates — a dispersion wide enough that this desk does not trust any single multiple here. That volatility is itself informative: it reflects genuinely unstable underlying profitability in European graphic paper, which is precisely the problem this JV is trying to solve structurally.

Rating: NEUTRAL. The JV is a sensible structural response to a shrinking market and is financed and reasonably advanced, but Sappi's own earnings volatility makes it impossible to say with confidence whether the current share price already captures the synergy upside or is simply tracking a genuinely unstable earnings base; a story to revisit once FY2026 numbers and JV completion are both in hand.

Jubilee Metals Group (JBL) BULLISHReview #1
Wednesday, 8 Oct 2026 — Update on Large Waste Project Disposal
Agreeing to sell the Zambian Large Waste Project for US$35.0m to a preferred bidder — at what the company describes as a substantial premium to its original acquisition cost — while redirecting proceeds toward core copper-processing growth at Molefe Mine, is a value-accretive portfolio simplification rather than a distress sale, though the deal still has to clear a two-stage due-diligence process before cash changes hands.

The structure — a 10-day legal/regulatory confirmation stage, followed by a 45-day exclusivity period funded by a US$2.25m payment, with the US$35m balance payable over up to three years (or US$30m if settled within two) — is a conservative, de-risked way to monetise a non-core asset; Jubilee gets an early exclusivity payment and retains some optionality if the buyer wants to accelerate. Framing this as "progressive derisking" of the large-growth portfolio, in favour of the smaller, already-producing Zambian copper operations, is a sensible capital-allocation signal for a company that has previously stretched across several growth projects simultaneously.

JBL's own trading multiples are hard to use productively: P/E estimates found range from roughly -4x (reflecting a loss-making period) to the mid-40s (a different, profitable period), while a price-to-NAV near 0.6x suggests the shares trade below book even as a peer comparison flags the stock as expensive on an earnings basis. That combination — expensive on earnings, cheap on book — is typical of an early-stage miner where earnings aren't yet the right lens; the waste-project sale, once it completes, will be a cleaner one-off data point on whether management is creating or destroying value through portfolio moves.

Rating: BULLISH. Monetising a non-core asset above its acquisition cost, with proceeds redirected to an already-producing core business, is the kind of capital discipline that's relatively rare among junior miners; the main risk is execution — confirming the two-stage due diligence completes and payments are received on schedule.

Disposal price: US$35.0m (or US$30m if settled within 2 years)  ·  Upfront exclusivity payment: US$2.25m  ·  Price-to-NAV: ~0.6x  ·  Trailing P/E: too dispersed to trust (ranged from negative to mid-40s across periods)

Alphamin Resources (APH) BULLISHReview #1
Tuesday, 6 Oct 2026 — Interim FY2026 Dividend / Q3 Guidance / Exploration Update
A steady 2% quarter-on-quarter rise in guided Q3 EBITDA to US$171m, a maintained interim dividend of C$0.13/share (~US$120m), and a high-grade exploration hit at Mpama South together describe a tin producer executing consistently through a cycle most peers would envy, at a multiple that — across a genuinely dispersed set of sources — still looks reasonable rather than demanding.

Production was essentially flat quarter-on-quarter (5,030 tonnes processed vs 5,046 tonnes sold) with ore grade easing only marginally (3.3% to 3.2% tin), so the EBITDA improvement is coming from price/cost management rather than a volume surge — a healthier combination than growth purely driven by throughput. Unit cost increases were mostly explained by royalties and export duties tied to higher tin prices themselves, plus some genuine diesel/logistics inflation, which is a less concerning cost story than an operational blow-out.

Declaring another interim dividend (payable 6 November) while guiding sequential EBITDA growth signals management isn't sacrificing shareholder returns to fund the ongoing Mpama South resource-expansion drilling, where the newly reported 19.29m at 5.76% Sn intercept is described as the widest, highest-grade hit of the current programme — genuine exploration upside on top of the production business. Reported trailing P/E figures found range roughly 7x–14x and price-to-NAV roughly 2x–3x depending on source and date; while too dispersed to pin to one number, even the higher end of that range isn't demanding for a company with this production consistency and dividend discipline.

Rating: BULLISH. Consistent quarter-on-quarter execution, a maintained dividend, and a high-grade exploration result together support the existing growth-and-income thesis; the key risk to monitor is tin-price sensitivity, given the group's own cost base is partly linked to royalties on the price itself.

Sirius Real Estate (SRE) NEUTRALReview #1
Monday, 5 Oct 2026 — Trading Update for the half year ended 30 September 2026
Rent-roll growth of 11.3% year-on-year (5.1% like-for-like) is a solid update, but it's a deceleration from the 15.2%/5.2% pace reported for the equivalent period a year earlier — still growing, just at a slightly slower rate — which is the right-sized read on a business management itself describes as operating in "a challenging macroeconomic and geopolitical environment."

The like-for-like figure (5.1% versus 5.2% a year ago) is essentially unchanged, meaning the deceleration in the headline 11.3% figure (from 15.2%) is almost entirely attributable to a slower pace of acquisitions rather than any weakening in the existing portfolio's organic performance — an important distinction, since organic like-for-like growth is the more durable, controllable driver and that line held up.

Acquisitive growth concentrated in Germany, alongside broadly similar organic growth across Germany and the UK, suggests a geographically balanced portfolio rather than reliance on a single market. On valuation, the clearest recent data point (a trailing P/E near 16.4x and dividend yield near 5.7%, dated mid-2026) sits against a dispersed set of price-to-NAV readings (roughly 0.9x–1.4x depending on source) — on the lower end of that range this would be an attractively priced income REIT, but the dispersion means this desk isn't confident enough in any single P/B figure to lean hard on it.

Rating: NEUTRAL. Still-growing like-for-like income and a maintained/growing dividend trajectory (per management's stated intent) are genuine positives, but the deceleration in headline rent-roll growth and the uncertainty around the current book-value multiple argue for waiting on the full half-year results (due 16 November) before forming a stronger view either way.
