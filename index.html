FirstRand (FSR) BULLISHReview #1
Thursday, 10 Sep 2026 — Audited results and ordinary cash dividend declaration for the year ended 30 June 2026
A record total dividend of 539 cents (+16%) is the headline, but it partly obscures the real story: FirstRand took a further GBP403m provision for the UK motor finance redress scheme and has now classified its UK operations as discontinued, so the number that matters is continuing-operations ROE of 24.9% — comfortably ahead of cost of equity — not the blended 18.3% group figure.

Normalised earnings grew 16% for the year, and headline ROE on a continuing basis came in at 21.5%, within FirstRand's target range; the blended group ROE of 18.3% is lower only because it still carries the drag of the discontinued UK business through to the reporting date. That split matters for reading the quality of this result — the core SA franchise (FNB retail, RMB investment banking, WesBank) is performing well above its cost of capital, while the UK motor-finance liability is a genuine, disclosed one-off being worked through rather than an ongoing drag on the go-forward business.

Balance sheet capital remains strong enough to fund both the record dividend and continued growth ambitions, including the recently approved NCBA stake in Kenya, which extends FirstRand's East African footprint. WesBank continues to flag Chinese used-vehicle resale-value risk as a watch item but remains profitable through it. At 12.25x trailing earnings and 2.22x price-to-book for a business generating continuing-operations ROE near 25%, the multiple looks reasonable rather than rich for the quality of the underlying franchise, particularly once the UK exit is fully worked through the accounts.

Rating: BULLISH. The continuing-operations returns are the number to anchor on, not the blended group figure still absorbing UK redress charges — a record, growing dividend and a bank generating returns well above its cost of equity is the more durable signal here. What would change the view: further escalation in UK redress liabilities beyond what's already provided, or signs the SA consumer credit cycle is turning against FNB and WesBank.

The growth is real and best-in-class, but the valuation has run well ahead of even these excellent fundamentals; this isn't a value opportunity at current multiples despite the quality of the franchise, and the credit-loss trend in the full interim results is the next thing to watch closely.
